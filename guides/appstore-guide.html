The big picture

The iPhone app is a small "shell" app that opens unicornergame.lol inside it, made with the same free tool we used for Android (PWABuilder). Everything you change on the website shows up in the app straight away, with no new app version needed.

The one thing the website can't do inside an iPhone app is take payments with Stripe: Apple's rules say digital things bought in an app must be bought with Apple's payments. So inside the app:

  • The Stripe buttons are hidden (already done for Android and iPhone).
  • Apple's own buy buttons appear instead. Tapping one shows Apple's payment sheet (Face ID, one tap).
  • Apple tells our server (Supabase) "this person paid", and our server unlocks Infinity on their Unicorner account. So it also works on the website and on Android.
Money flowPlayer pays Apple → Apple keeps 15% (Small Business Program) → Apple pays you monthly, about 45 days after the end of each month, into the bank account you add in Part 4.

Part 1: Your Apple ID

You need an Apple ID (the account you use for the App Store and iCloud) with two-factor authentication switched on. Apple requires it for developers.

  1. YOU On your Mac, click the Apple menu (the apple logo, top-left of the screen) → System Settings.
  2. Click your name at the top of the left sidebar. If you're not signed in, click Sign in and sign in with your Apple ID (or create one).
  3. Click Sign-In & Security. Check that Two-Factor Authentication says On. If it doesn't, click it and follow the steps (it texts a code to your phone).
Which Apple ID?Use the Apple ID you want to own the app for years. Your name (as the "seller") appears on the App Store page as the developer, for example "Developer: Pepe Asalas". You can't easily change this later. If you set up a company one day, the app can be transferred to it.

Part 2: Join the Apple Developer Program

  1. YOU On your Mac, open Safari and go to developer.apple.com/programs/enroll.
  2. Click Start Your Enrollment and sign in with your Apple ID.
  3. The easiest way is the Apple Developer app: if the page suggests it, install "Apple Developer" from the App Store on your iPhone or Mac, open it, tap Account → Enroll Now. It scans your ID and is usually approved faster.
  4. When asked for the entity type, choose Individual / Sole Proprietor. (Only choose "Organization" if you have a registered company. That needs a "D-U-N-S number" and takes weeks.)
  5. Fill in your legal name and address exactly as on your ID.
  6. Accept the Apple Developer Agreement.
  7. Pay the $99 (or the price in your currency). It renews every year; if you stop paying, the app disappears from the store.
  8. WAIT Apple checks your identity. You get an email "Welcome to the Apple Developer Program" (usually within 48 hours). If Apple asks for a photo of your ID, send it from the Apple Developer app.
While you waitDo Part 3 (install Xcode). Parts 4 onwards need the approved account.

Part 3: Install Xcode and the tools

Xcode is Apple's free program for making apps. It's big (about 10–15 GB), so start the download early.

  1. YOU Check your Mac's macOS version: Apple menu → About This Mac. Xcode needs a recent macOS. If it's old, first do Apple menu → System Settings → General → Software Update.
  2. Open the App Store app on your Mac, search for Xcode, click Get then Install. Make sure you have at least 40 GB of free space.
  3. When it finishes, open Xcode (it's in your Applications folder). The first time, it asks to install extra components: click Install and enter your Mac password. If it asks which platforms to download, tick iOS.
  4. In Xcode, click Xcode (top menu bar) → Settings… → Accounts tab → the + button at the bottom left → Apple ID → sign in with your developer Apple ID. Close the window.

CocoaPods (a helper the PWABuilder project uses)

The PWABuilder iPhone project uses a helper called CocoaPods. You install it once by typing a command in Terminal:

  1. YOU Press Cmd + Space, type Terminal, press Enter. A window with text opens: that's Terminal.
  2. Copy this line, paste it in Terminal and press Enter. It installs Homebrew (a free installer for developer tools). It asks for your Mac password: type it (nothing appears as you type, that's normal) and press Enter. It takes a few minutes.

    /bin/bash -c "$(curl -fsSL https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Homebrew/install/HEAD/install.sh)"

    At the end, Homebrew prints a "Next steps" section with two or three lines starting with echo and eval: copy and run those too (one at a time).
  3. Then run: brew install cocoapods
  4. Check it worked: pod --version should print a number like 1.16.2.
If something errorsCopy the whole Terminal window text (Cmd + A, Cmd + C) and paste it to Claude. Don't worry about breaking anything: these commands only install tools.

Part 4: Paid Apps agreement, bank and tax

Apple won't let you sell anything (not even test it properly) until this part is finished, and Apple takes a few days to approve it. Do it as soon as your developer account is approved.

  1. YOU Go to appstoreconnect.apple.com and sign in. This website is App Store Connect: where you manage the app, prices and sales.
  2. Click Business (on the home screen; in some versions it's called Agreements, Tax, and Banking).
  3. You'll see a Paid Apps agreement. Click View and Agree to Terms, read, tick, Agree. (The "Free Apps" one is already active.)
  4. Bank account: click Add Bank Account (or Set Up next to Banking). Choose your bank's country, enter the account holder name exactly as at the bank, and your IBAN (European banks) or account and routing numbers. Apple may ask for the bank's SWIFT/BIC code: it's on your bank's website or app.
  5. Tax forms: click Set Up Tax Forms (or Tax Forms → Add). Apple asks if you're a US person:
    • If you're not a US citizen or resident: fill in the W-8BEN. It asks your country of residence and, if available, your tax number (in Spain your NIF/DNI; in the Netherlands your BSN). Claiming "treaty benefits" for your country reduces the US tax withheld on US sales: Apple's form guides you; for most European countries the royalty rate is 0%.
    • If you are a US person: fill in the W-9.
    Apple may also ask for tax forms for other countries (for example Australia, Brazil, Japan): follow the prompts, most are a few ticks.
  6. Contact info: add yourself as the Senior Management, Finance, Technical and Legal contact (you can be all of them).
  7. WAIT The Paid Apps status changes from "Processing" to Active, usually in 1–3 days. You can carry on with Parts 5 to 9 meanwhile.
Is this safe?Yes: this is how every app developer gets paid. Apple sends you a payment each month (if you earned at least the minimum, about $10–150 depending on the country), with a sales report.

Part 5: The Small Business Program

Normally Apple keeps 30% of sales. If you earn under $1 million a year from the App Store, you can pay just 15%. You must apply: it isn't automatic.

  1. YOU Go to developer.apple.com/app-store/small-business-program.
  2. Click Enroll / Apply now, sign in, and fill in the short form (it asks if you have other developer accounts: answer No, unless you do).
  3. WAIT Apple confirms by email. The 15% applies from the date you're accepted, so apply before you start selling.

Part 8: The In-App Purchase key and purchase notifications

Our server needs a key to ask Apple "did this person really pay?". You create it, download it once, and store it in Supabase. Never paste the key into a chat or email: it's like a password.

8a. Create the key

  1. YOU App Store Connect → Users and Access (top menu) → Integrations tab → In-App Purchase (left list) → Generate In-App Purchase Key (or the +).
  2. Name: Unicorner server → Generate.
  3. Write down two values shown on that page (they're not secret, you can send these to Claude):
    • Key ID (10 characters, e.g. 2X9R4HXF34)
    • Issuer ID (a long code with dashes, shown above the list)
  4. Click Download. You get a file like SubscriptionKey_2X9R4HXF34.p8. You can only download it once. Keep it somewhere safe (e.g. a "Unicorner keys" folder, and a backup).

8b. Put the key in Supabase

  1. YOU Go to supabase.com/dashboard → your Unicorner project → Edge Functions (left sidebar) → Secrets (or Manage secrets).
  2. Add three secrets (name exactly as written, value as described):
    NameValue
    APPLE_IAP_KEY_IDthe Key ID
    APPLE_IAP_ISSUER_IDthe Issuer ID
    APPLE_IAP_PRIVATE_KEYopen the .p8 file with TextEdit (right-click → Open With → TextEdit), select all, copy, paste. It starts with -----BEGIN PRIVATE KEY-----.
  3. Click Save. Then tell Claude "the Apple secrets are in Supabase" (don't send the values).

Part 10: What Claude builds

When you reach this point, tell Claude "I'm at Part 10". Claude does all of this; you just test it afterwards.

1. Apple payments in the appA small piece of native iPhone code (Apple's "StoreKit 2") added to the PWABuilder project: it loads the two products with Apple's local prices, shows Apple's payment sheet, and hands the receipt to the website. Plus a Restore purchases button, which Apple requires.
2. The server function (apple-iap)In Supabase: receives the purchase from the app, double-checks it with Apple using your key (Part 8), and unlocks Infinity on the player's account, the same way Stripe does today. It also receives Apple's notifications (renewals, cancellations, refunds).
3. Website changes for the iPhone app
  • Inside the app, the Infinity screen shows Apple's buy buttons and prices instead of Stripe (Stripe stays on the website).
  • Google sign-in is hidden in the app: Google blocks it inside iPhone apps. Players sign in with email (a code or password), which works.
  • "Delete my account" button: Apple requires apps with accounts to let people delete them from inside the app.
  • A short line on the Infinity screen: "Already bought Infinity on the website? Sign in and it's unlocked here too."
Why sign-in is required to buyInfinity is tied to the Unicorner account, so it works on the website, Android and iPhone. Apple allows asking people to sign in before buying when the purchase is linked to an account.

Part 11: Open the app in Xcode and run it on your iPhone

11a. Open the project

  1. YOU Double-click the zip Claude sent back to unzip it. Inside there's a folder (e.g. src) with a file ending in .xcworkspace.
  2. Open Terminal, type cd (with a space after), then drag the folder that contains the Podfile from Finder into the Terminal window, and press Enter.
  3. Run pod install and wait until it says "Pod installation complete".
  4. In Finder, double-click the .xcworkspace file (the white icon, not the .xcodeproj). Xcode opens the project.

11b. Signing (tells Apple the app is yours)

  1. In Xcode's left column, click the top item (the blue project icon with the app's name).
  2. In the middle, under TARGETS, click the app (e.g. "Unicorner"). Open the Signing & Capabilities tab.
  3. Tick Automatically manage signing. Team: choose your name (Personal Team won't work; it must say your name with "(Individual)" or similar, from the developer account). Bundle Identifier: lol.unicornergame.ios.
  4. Click + Capability (top left of that tab) and add In-App Purchase (if it isn't there already).
  5. Open the General tab: Display Name Unicorner, Version 1.0, Build 1. Under Supported Destinations, keep iPhone only (remove iPad and Mac if listed: otherwise Apple wants iPad screenshots too).

11c. Run it on your iPhone

  1. Plug your iPhone into the Mac with the cable. On the iPhone tap Trust This Computer and enter your passcode.
  2. On the iPhone: Settings → Privacy & Security → scroll to the bottom → Developer Mode → turn on → restart when asked → confirm Turn On after restarting. (This option appears only after the iPhone has been connected to Xcode once.)
  3. In Xcode, at the top centre, click the device menu and choose your iPhone. Then press the ▶ Run button (top left), or Cmd + R.
  4. The first time, the iPhone may say "Untrusted Developer": on the iPhone go to Settings → General → VPN & Device Management → your developer name → Trust. Press ▶ again.
  5. Unicorner opens on your iPhone. Play a game, check sign-in works, and check there are no Stripe buttons.
Red errors in Xcode?Take a screenshot (Cmd + Shift + 4, then drag over the error) and send it to Claude. Most first-time errors are signing settings and take one click to fix.

Part 12: Test buying with a free Sandbox account

Apple has a test mode called Sandbox: you "buy" with a fake test account and no money is charged. Subscriptions renew every few minutes instead of every month, so you can test renewals quickly.

  1. YOU App Store Connect → Users and Access → Sandbox tab (or "Sandbox Testers") → +.
  2. Fill in a name and an email address that is not already an Apple ID. A trick: if your email is name@gmail.com, use name+sandbox1@gmail.com (it still arrives in your inbox). Choose a password and your country. Create.
  3. On your iPhone: Settings → Developer (appears after Part 11) → Sandbox Apple Account → sign in with the test account. (On some versions it's under Settings → App Store → Sandbox Account.)
  4. Run the app from Xcode (▶). Sign in to Unicorner with your normal Unicorner email. Open Infinity and tap Infinity forever. Apple's sheet appears with "[Environment: Sandbox]". Confirm.
  5. Infinity should unlock within a few seconds. Then also check it shows as unlocked on the website (same Unicorner account) on your computer.
  6. Test Restore purchases: delete the app from the iPhone, run it again from Xcode, sign in, tap Restore purchases.
  7. Test the monthly one with a second sandbox account (or after cancelling): it should renew every ~5 minutes for a few cycles, then stop.
NotePurchases only work once the Paid Apps agreement (Part 4) is Active. If the buy buttons say "unavailable", that's usually the reason.

Part 13: Upload the app to Apple

  1. YOU In Xcode, at the top, change the device menu from your iPhone to Any iOS Device (arm64).
  2. Menu bar: Product → Archive. It builds for a minute or two, then the Organizer window opens with your archive.
  3. Click Distribute App → App Store Connect → Upload (or "Distribute") → keep the default options → Next until Upload.
  4. WAIT 10–30 minutes. Apple emails you when the build has finished "processing". In App Store Connect → your app → TestFlight tab you'll see build 1.0 (1).
  5. If asked about Export Compliance (encryption): answer that the app uses only standard encryption (HTTPS) → it's exempt. Usually: "None of the algorithms mentioned above" / "Standard encryption".

Optional: TestFlight (let friends test before launch)

  1. TestFlight tab → Internal Testing → + → add yourself. Install the TestFlight app on your iPhone; you get an invite to install Unicorner from there. Good for checking the real downloaded app.
Every new upload needs a higher Build numberNext time: General tab → Build 2, then 3, and so on. Version stays 1.0 until you release it.

14f. App Privacy ("privacy nutrition label")

Left sidebar → App Privacy → Get Started. Privacy policy URL: https://unicornergame.lol/privacy.html. Then: "Do you collect data?" → Yes. Tick:

  • Contact Info → Email Address: used for App Functionality (account), linked to the user, not used for tracking.
  • Identifiers → User ID: App Functionality, linked, not tracking.
  • Purchases → Purchase History: App Functionality, linked, not tracking.
  • Usage Data → Product Interaction: Analytics and App Functionality, not tracking (game events, scores).
  • User Content → Other User Content (chat messages, nicknames): App Functionality, linked.

"Tracking" means sharing data with other companies for ads. Unicorner doesn't do that in the app, so answer No to tracking. If that changes (for example ads), this must be updated.

Bundle IDThe app's unique ID, written like a reversed web address.
Build / ArchiveA packaged copy of the app ready to upload. Each upload needs a new build number.
In-App Purchase (IAP)Buying something inside the app with Apple's payments.
Non-consumableA one-time purchase you keep forever (Infinity forever).
Auto-renewable subscriptionCharges every month until cancelled (Infinity monthly).
SandboxApple's free test mode for purchases: no real money.
TestFlightApple's app for installing test versions before release.
PWABuilderThe free tool that turns the website into an app (we used it for Android).
SupabaseUnicorner's database and server, where accounts and Infinity live.
TerminalThe Mac app where you type commands (Cmd + Space, type Terminal).
.p8 keyThe secret key file that lets our server check purchases with Apple. Treat like a password.
Stuck anywhere?Send Claude a screenshot (Cmd + Shift + 4 and drag over the area; the picture appears on your Desktop) and say which Part and step you're on.
